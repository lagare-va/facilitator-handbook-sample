What the handbook contains

Day-by-day master timelines

Each day has a minute-by-minute schedule — opening ceremony, obstacle blocks, break timing, free sail activities, debrief, and pack-up. Designed so a facilitator with no prior context can run the day independently by following the timeline alone.

Full facilitator scripts

Every day opens with a themed story read aloud to the group — written from Luffy's perspective and designed to build narrative tension across all 5 days. Scripts include exact wording, physical cues, and how to handle audience reaction.

Obstacle instructions with rules & scoring

Each physical obstacle (Jenga, Connect 4, Clay Sculpt, Dixit, Word Search, Relay Race) has its own setup guide, play rules, win conditions, coin rewards, and age-specific facilitator notes for 8–9 vs 11–13 year olds.

Debrief questions & curriculum connections

Every obstacle ends with a reflection question and a labeled connection to the digital session happening the same day — so the offline and online halves of the program reinforce each other intentionally.

Troubleshooting guide — energy, conflict, pacing, inclusion

Covers 12 specific scenarios: energy drops, teams rushing ahead, a child being excluded, mixed age dynamics (8 vs 13), running ahead or behind schedule, materials failing mid-activity, and conflict over scores. Each scenario has a concrete, named response — not generic advice.

Scoring system — coin accumulation across 5 days

AchievementCoins earned
Completing the day's obstacle(s)+10
Fastest team to finish+5

Best team spirit / sportsmanship+3
Winning a head-to-head duel+2

Scoreboard updated publicly after each day. Every student receives a certificate and prize regardless of final score — the handbook explicitly states no child leaves as a loser.

What I built and why it matters

I designed this handbook from scratch — not from a template, and not from a brief someone else wrote. I was given the program dates, the age group, and the One Piece theme. Everything else — the 5-day arc, the obstacle mechanics, the scoring system, the facilitator scripts, the debrief questions, and the troubleshooting guide — I built independently.

The handbook was designed so that any facilitator who had never run the program before could pick it up the night before and be ready the next day. That meant writing scripts in full, not just outlines. It meant anticipating exactly where things go wrong with kids aged 8–13 and writing specific responses, not generic tips. It meant sequencing the five days so each one built on the last — the clay islands from Day 3 reappear in the Day 4 relay, the island drawings from Day 2 become the Day 3 sculpt blueprints, the map pieces from all 5 days assemble into a single reveal moment on Day 4.

I use this as a portfolio piece not because it is a children's program — but because it demonstrates what I do in every operational role: take an ambiguous brief, build the complete structure behind it, document it clearly enough that others can execute it, and deliver it on time and under budget.
